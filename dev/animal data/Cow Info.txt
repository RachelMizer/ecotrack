Cow Info

AVERAGE TEMPERATURE
A healthy adult cow has a normal internal body temperature of 100.4°F to 102.8°F (38°C to 39.3°C), averaging around 101.5°F.

Body Temperature Details
Internal Core:  
Adult cattle maintain a core temperature between 100.4°F and 102.8°F.
Calves run slightly warmer, often 101°F to 103°F, due to higher metabolic rates.

Skin Temperature:  
Typically 5°F to 10°F lower than core temperature depending on coat thickness, weather, and hydration.

Danger Zones:

Below 100°F: Hypothermia risk, especially in newborn calves.

Above 103°F: Early heat stress.

Above 106°F: Severe heat stress; 107°F+ can be fatal without rapid cooling.

Ideal Environmental (Air) Temperatures
Comfort Range:  
Cattle thrive in cooler climates, ideally between 40°F and 75°F (4°C to 24°C).

Cold Tolerance:  
Adult cows tolerate cold extremely well, even down to -20°F with wind protection, dry bedding, and adequate feed.
Calves are more vulnerable and require shelter and warmth.

Heat Tolerance:  
Temperatures above 80°F–85°F begin causing heat stress.
Above 90°F, cows require shade, airflow, and abundant water.
High humidity dramatically increases heat‑stress risk.

AVERAGE LIFESPAN
Cows typically live 15 to 20 years, though lifespan varies significantly based on breed, purpose, and care.

Factors Affecting Lifespan
Breed:  
Hardy breeds like Angus, Hereford, and Longhorns often live longer than high‑production dairy breeds.

Diet and Water:  
Balanced nutrition, mineral access, and constant clean water support long-term health.

Protection:  
Secure fencing, predator control, and shelter from extreme weather prevent injury and early mortality.

Health Care:  
Routine vaccinations, parasite control, hoof care, and reproductive management extend lifespan.

AGE
Cows reach adulthood when they achieve sexual maturity, typically between 10 and 14 months, though full physical maturity comes later.

Milestones of Adulthood
Heifers (Females):  
Reach puberty around 9–12 months, depending on breed and nutrition.
Considered adults when they calve for the first time, usually around 24 months.

Bulls (Males):  
Reach sexual maturity around 10–12 months, showing mounting and breeding behaviors.
Full physical maturity occurs around 2–3 years.

Full Size:  
While sexually mature early, cattle continue growing until 3–4 years of age, especially larger beef breeds.
